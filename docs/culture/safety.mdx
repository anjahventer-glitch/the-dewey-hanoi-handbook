# Safety

Hanoi is generally considered a safe city for both locals and expatriates. Like any large city, it is important to stay aware of your surroundings and take sensible precautions.

---

## Personal Safety

- Keep valuables out of sight in busy areas.
- Be aware of your surroundings when using your phone in public.
- Use well-lit streets when walking at night.
- Avoid carrying large amounts of cash.

---

## Traffic Safety

Traffic can be one of the biggest adjustments for newcomers.

- Look carefully before crossing the road.
- Be aware of motorbikes approaching from different directions.
- Wear a helmet whenever riding a motorbike or bicycle.
- Always use seatbelts where available.

---

## Scams

While most people are friendly and honest, be aware of common tourist scams.

Examples include:

- Overpriced taxis (use Grab or reputable taxi companies instead).
- Inflated prices in tourist areas.

---

## Drinking Water

Tap water in Hanoi is **not considered safe to drink**.

Most residents and expats use:

- Bottled water
- Filtered water
- Refillable water dispensers

Most apartment buildings can recommend a local water delivery company, so ask your landlord, building management or colleagues for advice. Many suppliers offer convenient home delivery of 19-litre bottles and simply exchange your empty bottle for a full one during each delivery.
> 💡 **Boiling tap water is not enough.** While boiling kills bacteria and other microorganisms, it does **not** remove dissolved contaminants such as heavy metals or minerals. It's best to use filtered or bottled water for drinking, making tea or coffee, cooking and brushing your teeth.

Some expats also find that Hanoi's hard water can affect their hair and skin over time. As a result, some people choose to wash their hair with filtered or distilled water, particularly if they have colour-treated or sensitive hair.

---

## Natural Hazards

- Summer storms can cause local flooding.
- Strong sun and high humidity can lead to dehydration.
- Check air quality during winter before spending long periods outdoors.

---

## Tips

- Trust your instincts.
- Share your location with friends or family when travelling alone.
- Save your emergency contacts in your phone.
- Ask colleagues if you're unsure about an area or activity.
