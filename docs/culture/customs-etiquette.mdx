# Customs & Etiquette

Vietnamese people are known for being welcoming, respectful and community-oriented. Understanding a few local customs will help you settle in quickly and build positive relationships.

---

## Greetings

- A smile is the most common greeting.
- Handshakes are common in professional settings.
- Address older people respectfully.

---

## Shoes

- It is common to remove your shoes before entering someone's home and some businesses.
- If you see shoes at the entrance, remove yours as well.

---

## Public Behaviour

- Speak politely and avoid raising your voice.
- Public displays of anger are generally frowned upon.
- Queue patiently and be respectful of others.

---

## Giving & Receiving

When giving or receiving something, especially from an older person, it is polite to use both hands where possible.

---

## Temples & Pagodas

When visiting religious sites:

- Dress modestly.
- Speak quietly.
- Remove hats where appropriate.
- Follow any posted rules.

---

## Dining Etiquette

- Wait for everyone to be served before eating.
- Sharing dishes is common.
- Chopsticks should never be left standing upright in a bowl of rice.

---

## Photography

- Always ask permission before taking close-up photos of people, especially children or monks.

---

## Tips

- Respect for elders is an important part of Vietnamese culture.
- Learning a few Vietnamese words is always appreciated.
- Be patient when navigating cultural differences—people are generally very understanding and helpful.
