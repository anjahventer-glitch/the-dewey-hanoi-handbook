# Motorbikes

Motorbikes are the most common form of transport in Hanoi. Many teachers choose to rent or buy one once they become familiar with the city's roads and traffic.

---

## Should You Ride?

If you've never ridden a motorbike before, it's recommended that you spend some time using Grab before deciding to ride yourself.

Hanoi traffic can seem overwhelming at first, but most people become much more comfortable after a few weeks.

---

## Renting

Renting is a great option if you're staying for a shorter period or would like to gain confidence before buying.

## Buying

Many long-term teachers eventually choose to buy their own scooter.

Before purchasing:

- Ask colleagues for recommendations.
- Check that the bike has valid registration papers (Blue Card).
- Test ride the bike.
- Purchase from a reputable dealer whenever possible.

---

## Motorcycle Licence

To legally ride a motorbike in Vietnam, you must have a valid Vietnamese motorcycle licence.

### Important Information

- Vietnam does **not** recognise International Driving Permits (IDPs) for residents.
- If you already have a motorcycle licence from your home country, it can usually be translated and converted into a Vietnamese licence.
- If you do not already hold a motorcycle licence, you'll need to complete the Vietnamese motorcycle licence test, which is conducted in Vietnamese.
- Vietnamese licences are linked to your Temporary Residence Card (TRC). When your TRC expires or is renewed, you'll need to apply for a new Vietnamese licence.
- While some police officers may choose not to stop foreign riders because of the additional paperwork involved, this should never be relied upon. Traffic laws are enforced, and you should always carry the correct licence, registration and insurance.

> 💡 **Tip:** If you want someone to accompany you to the DMV contact Duc Trung - 098 397 5790. He will help you for a fee. (more info under resources)
---

## Safety

- Always wear a quality helmet.
- Carry your licence and bike registration.
- Ride defensively and expect the unexpected.
- Never use your phone while riding.
- Take extra care in wet weather.
- Build your confidence gradually by riding quieter roads before tackling busy intersections.
